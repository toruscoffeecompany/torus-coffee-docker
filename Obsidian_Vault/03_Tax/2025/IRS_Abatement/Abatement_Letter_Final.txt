1. TIMELY MAILING UNDER IRC §7502

The partnership's 2025 Form 1065 was sent via U.S. Certified Mail (Tracking No.
9589 0710 5270 2716 3052 78) on March 13, 2026, three days before the return's
due date of March 16, 2026 (March 15 fell on a Sunday). Under IRC §7502, the
postmark date of a properly addressed return sent by U.S. mail is treated as the
filing date, regardless of the date the return is received or processed by the
Service.

USPS tracking records confirm the package was in USPS possession in Iowa City,
IA on March 13, 2026 at 3:49 PM, and was delivered to the Service's Ogden, UT
facility on March 20, 2026 at 12:30 PM. We believe this seven-day transit
period — not any delay on the partnership's part — is the reason the return
appears late in the Service's records. A copy of the certified mail receipt and
the USPS tracking history is enclosed as proof of timely mailing. On this basis,
the return was filed on time and no failure-to-file penalty should apply.

2. IN THE ALTERNATIVE: REVENUE PROCEDURE 84-35

Should the Service determine any penalty is nonetheless applicable, the
partnership independently qualifies for relief under Rev. Proc. 84-35:

a. The partnership had only two partners during the 2025 tax year.

b. Both partners are individuals.

c. Both partners timely filed income tax returns on which all partnership
distributive items were fully and properly reported.

d. Each partner's percentage share of income, loss, and every other partnership
item was identical throughout the year for that partner (Bryon Scott Smith: 49%;
Sara Jane Shelvock: 51%) across all distributive items.

e. The partnership has not made an election to be subject to the consolidated
audit procedures under Subchapter C of Chapter 63 of the Internal Revenue Code.

We declare under penalties of perjury that the partnership meets each of the
criteria stated above.
