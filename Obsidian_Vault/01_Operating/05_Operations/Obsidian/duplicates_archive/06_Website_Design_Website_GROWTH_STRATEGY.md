## Phase 3: Land Your First Retail Account (3+ Months)

### A. Know Your Ask
Before contacting stores, decide:
- Which products in which sizes?
- What wholesale price works for you?
- Minimum order quantity?
- Can you deliver or do they pick up?
- Shelf life guarantee?

### B. Target Smart
Start with:
- **Local Iowa City first** - Build your home base
- **Independent groceries** - More willing to stock local than chains
- **Co-ops & health food stores** - Love the freeze-dried/wellness angle
- **Coffee shops** - Perfect match for your brand!
- **Farmers markets with vendor slots** - Step up from flea markets

### C. Website as Social Proof
When you call/visit stores, mention:
- "You can see our full product line at [website]"
- "We're growing—already active at [flea market]"
- "We handle custom orders and wholesale"

This makes you look like a real business, not a side hustle.

## What To Avoid

❌ Don't go silent on flea markets while pursuing retail
- Grocery stores want to see *active sales*
- Use flea markets as your proof of demand

❌ Don't oversell your production capacity
- Be honest about what you can supply
- Growing gradually is better than over-promising

❌ Don't ignore your website once it's live
- Update flea market locations monthly
- Add new products as you create them
- Respond to inquiries within 24 hours

## Pricing Strategy (Think B2B)

When grocery stores ask about wholesale pricing, think:

**Your cost: $X per unit**
**Your retail price: $Y (markup ~2-3x cost)**
**Wholesale price: $Y × 0.40-0.50** (40-50% of retail)
= Grocery store gets margin, you make profit

Example:
- Cost to make: $1.50
- Retail price: $5.99
- Wholesale to store: ~$3.00
- Store sells for $5.99, keeps ~$2.99 profit

Be prepared to discuss this!
