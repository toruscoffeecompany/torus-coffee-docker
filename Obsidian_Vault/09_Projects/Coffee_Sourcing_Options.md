## Options

### Option A: Buy from Distributor (Recommended for Launch)
**Pros:**
- No equipment investment
- Consistent quality
- Lower risk
- Fastest time to market

**Cons:**
- Lower margins
- Less control over sourcing
- Dependent on supplier availability

**Estimated Costs:**
- Green beans: $3-$8/lb wholesale
- Roasted beans: $5-$12/lb wholesale
- Minimum order: 50-100 lbs
- Setup: $0

**Potential Distributors:**
- [ ] Sweet Maria's (green beans, online)
- [ ] Royal Coffee (green beans, bulk)
- [ ] Local Iowa roasters (partnership)
- [ ] Midwest Coffee Distributors

**Decision factors:**
- Cost per pound
- Minimum order quantity
- Lead time
- Roast profile options
- Organic/fair trade certification

### Option B: Roast Own Beans
**Pros:**
- Higher margins
- Full control over quality
- Unique selling proposition
- Can create signature blends

**Cons:**
- Equipment investment: $2,000-$10,000
- Learning curve
- Time commitment
- Quality consistency challenges

**Estimated Costs:**
- Small roaster (1-2 lb): $2,000-$5,000
- Medium roaster (5-10 lb): $5,000-$15,000
- Green beans: $3-$8/lb
- Ventilation/permits: $500-$2,000
- Training/certification: $500-$1,000

**Equipment options:**
- [ ] Aillio Bullet R1 (electric, $2,000)
- [ ] Mill City Roasters (various sizes)
- [ ] Used commercial roaster ($3,000-$8,000)

### Option C: Hybrid Approach
- Start with distributor for consistency
- Invest in small roaster for special batches
- Use distributor as backup during equipment issues

## Financial Comparison

| Factor | Distributor | Roast Own |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Startup cost | $0 | $2,000-$15,000 |
| Cost per lb | $5-$12 | $3-$8 + labor |
| Time to market | 1-2 weeks | 1-3 months |
| Control | Low | High |
| Risk | Low | High |

## Recommendation
**Phase 1:** Start with distributor (Option A) to validate demand
**Phase 2:** Invest in roaster when sales justify equipment cost
