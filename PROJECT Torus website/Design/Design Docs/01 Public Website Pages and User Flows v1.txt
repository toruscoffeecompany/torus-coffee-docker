Torus Coffee Company - Public Website Pages and User Flows
Last updated: 2026-05-29

Purpose
This document defines the public-facing website design: pages, content, navigation, calls to action, and customer flows.

Primary Website Goal
Help people confidently buy Torus Coffee Company products online.

Secondary Goals
- Build trust after a low-sales first year.
- Make the business feel active, real, local, and handmade.
- Prepare for wholesale/grocery expansion later.
- Support blogs, recipes, social sharing, and product discovery.
- Create a strong base for Google, Pinterest, Meta, and SEO.

Brand Positioning
Name:
Torus Coffee Company

Primary tagline:
Stay Curious. Stay Crunchy. Stay Cosmic.

Plain-language explanation:
Freeze-dried candy, fruit, snacks, and cosmic kitchen experiments from Iowa.

Brand feel:
- Cosmic
- Playful
- Slightly witchy/magical
- Midwest-made
- Warm and friendly
- Clear and trustworthy
- Handmade, not corporate

Visual Direction
Use the existing logo/business card as the anchor.

Core visual ingredients:
- Midnight navy / space background
- Cream/off-white surfaces
- Gold accents
- Purple and teal cosmic accents
- Real product photography
- Star, moon, orbit, constellation, and subtle sparkle motifs

Important design balance:
The site can be whimsical, but the shopping path must be very clear. Customers should never have to decode the brand language to understand what a product is, how much it costs, whether it is in stock, or how to buy it.

Primary Navigation
Recommended launch navigation:
- Shop
- Blog
- About
- Find Us
- Contact
- Cart/Account later, when full checkout/accounts exist

Mobile navigation:
- Sticky top bar with logo and menu button
- Clear Shop button or icon
- Menu opens full-screen or drawer style
- Buy buttons must be thumb-friendly

Footer Navigation
Footer should include:
- Shop
- About
- Blog
- Contact
- Shipping Policy
- Refund & Returns Policy
- Privacy Policy
- Terms & Conditions
- Accessibility Statement
- Social links
- Email and phone
- Copyright

Public Page List

1. Home
Purpose:
Introduce the brand quickly and move shoppers toward products.

Recommended sections:
1. Hero
   - Logo/brand signal
   - Tagline: Stay Curious. Stay Crunchy. Stay Cosmic.
   - Plain explanation: Freeze-dried candy, fruit, snacks, and cosmic kitchen experiments from Iowa.
   - Primary button: Shop Snacks
   - Secondary button: Read The Orbit Report or Find Us Locally
   - Real product/brand image or strong cosmic product visual

2. Featured Products
   - 4 to 8 launch products
   - Product image, name, price, short description, and Buy/Details button
   - Manual featured flag controlled by admin later

3. Why Freeze-Dried?
   - Short, visual explanation of crunch, flavor, shelf life, and snack fun
   - Keep educational but not too technical

4. Product Categories
   - Freeze-Dried Candy
   - Freeze-Dried Fruit
   - Seasonal
   - Gift Boxes / Variety Packs later

5. Trust/Proof Section
   - Iowa-made
   - Small batch
   - Secure checkout through Square
   - U.S. shipping only
   - Real contact info

6. Blog Teaser
   - The Orbit Report
   - The Orbit Workshop
   - The Orbit Kitchen
   - Show latest posts once blog exists

7. Email Signup
   - Restocks, launches, events, recipes, and cosmic snack news
   - Consent text required

8. Social/Events Teaser
   - Follow on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, YouTube as appropriate
   - Link to Find Us page

Home page success criteria:
- Visitor understands what Torus sells within 5 seconds.
- Shop button is obvious.
- Brand personality is present but not confusing.
- Mobile layout is clean and fast.

2. Shop Page
Purpose:
Let customers browse all active products.

Recommended features:
- Product grid
- Category filter
- Search/filter later
- Sort later: featured, newest, price, in stock
- Product cards with image, name, price, category, stock badge, short description, and Details/Buy button

Product card fields:
- Main image
- Product name
- Price
- Badge: New, Best Seller, Seasonal, Low Stock, Sold Out
- Short description
- Button: View Product
- Optional direct Buy button if Square link exists

Shop page filters for launch:
- All
- Candy
- Fruit
- Seasonal, if any active products

Future filters:
- Sour
- Chocolatey
- Fruity
- Giftable
- In stock
- Price range

Out-of-stock behavior:
Default: keep products visible, mark Sold Out, disable Buy button, optionally show Notify Me later.

3. Product Detail Page
Purpose:
Give customers enough confidence to buy.

Required sections:
- Product name
- Main product image
- Image gallery
- Price
- Availability badge
- Short description
- Buy with Square button
- Secure checkout note
- Size/weight
- Category
- Flavor/texture notes
- Ingredients/allergen note, if available
- Shelf life/storage note, if available
- Shipping note: Ships in the U.S. only
- Related products
- Social share links

Recommended copy style:
Use fun cosmic names, but also plain-language details.

Example structure:
Name: Aurora Bites 2.6oz
Plain description: Freeze-dried rainbow candy with a light, crunchy snap and fruity flavor.
Brand flavor line: A kaleidoscope of cosmic crunch from another dimension.

Buy button behavior in Phase 1:
- Button opens Square payment/checkout link in same tab or new tab, final decision later.
- Add note near button: Secure checkout powered by Square.
- If no Square link exists, show Contact to Order or Coming Soon.

Product page trust details:
- U.S. shipping only
- Secure Square checkout
- Real business email/phone
- Refund/shipping policy links

Build trust and emotional connection.

Need exported final text from existing Google Docs or new policy drafts.

Customer Flows

Flow 1: New shopper buys a product
1. Lands on home page.
2. Clicks Shop Snacks.
3. Browses Shop page.
4. Opens Product Detail.
5. Reviews photo, price, description, shipping note.
6. Clicks Buy with Square.
7. Completes payment/shipping in Square.
8. Receives Square confirmation.

Flow 2: Shopper discovers from Google free listing later
1. Searches Google.
2. Finds product free listing/rich result.
3. Clicks product page.
4. Buys through Square link.

Flow 3: Blog reader becomes buyer
1. Reads recipe or workshop post.
2. Sees relevant product callout.
3. Clicks product.
4. Buys or joins email list.

Flow 4: Local customer checks events
1. Opens Find Us.
2. Sees upcoming market.
3. Follows social or visits event.

Flow 5: Wholesale buyer evaluates business
1. Opens Home/About/Wholesale.
2. Checks product quality, story, contact info, policies.
3. Submits wholesale inquiry.

Design Risks
- Too much whimsy can hurt clarity.
- Too many future features can slow launch.
- Product pages without good photos will not convert well.
- Checkout handoff to Square must be explained clearly.

Design Requirements Summary
- Mobile-first.
- Fast loading.
- Real product images.
- Clear Shop path.
- Secure Square checkout messaging.
- Strong product pages.
- Blog-ready.
- Feed/SEO-ready.
- Easy to expand.
